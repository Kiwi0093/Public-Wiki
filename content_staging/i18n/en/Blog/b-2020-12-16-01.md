Lately I don't know what got into me, but I've become obsessed with installing Android on a PC again...

Following Phoenix OS, I found another Android x86 distribution that fits my expectations even better — Bliss OS.

<!--truncate-->

## [Bliss OS](https://blissos.org/)

This is an open-source version of Android x86, which inherently appeals to my tastes much more than Phoenix OS from a mainland company (okay, I admit I'm biased against mainland software...).

Even better, this team uses a Surface as their development machine, so Surface compatibility is quite good.

#### Base System

Like others, it comes in both x86 and x64 builds, and generally the x86 versions are based on older Android releases. What's thoughtful about this system, though, is that when downloading, it asks about the specs of the machine you plan to install it on and helps you pick the right ISO version—largely preventing the tragedy I ran into with Phoenix OS last time... (Though to be fair, before installing Phoenix OS I didn't know the T100 had so many limitations either...)

The current stable release is based on Android 9.

It installs super bare-bones and clean, which really wins me over.. (Yes! I do have this weird quirk.)

Though in mainland China that might not necessarily be a good thing.. since the GFW is sitting right there....

#### Linux Drivers for Surface

Basically it directly uses the Linux Surface drivers developed by [jakeday's linux-surface GitHub project](https://github.com/jakeday/linux-surface/tree/master/firmware). The painful part, however, is that you can't just pack this firmware directly into the ISO and call it a day.

Instead, you have to inject the files while the touchscreen isn't working yet and then reboot (speaking of which, thank goodness the Surface Pro 4 I tested on yesterday came with a full set of accessories like the dock and keyboard...).

## Test Results

First, true to a Surface Pro 4, the i5 CPU + 8GB of RAM runs very smoothly, and the installation went smoothly following the official website's guide (okay, nowadays I prefer to steer clear of machines that only support UEFI boot...).

Although the boot screen is pretty ugly—it'd be nice to customize it later...

However, optimization might still not be quite right: yesterday I found that if you boot with the dock attached, Wi-Fi doesn't work...

I'll leave the remaining minor quirks for the actual owner of the machine to poke at as they play with it....
