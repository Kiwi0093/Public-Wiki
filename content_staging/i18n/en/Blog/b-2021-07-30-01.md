# WireGuard

Yes, I didn't pick V2ray—which I knew inside and out in China—and instead went with WireGuard, which has direct support in the Linux Kernel. Here's why:

* Outside of China, there's no need for obfuscated connections
* WireGuard is now directly supported by the Linux Kernel
* WireGuard is simpler to configure (well... once you understand it, yeah, it really is)

However, because this protocol explicitly tells everyone "I'm a VPN," naturally, no matter how smoothly it runs in Taiwan, you can't use it as a ladder to climb the GFW.

If you want to bypass censorship, you're still better off sticking with something obfuscated like V2ray.

# Ramblings

## Wireguard-Manager

The first thing I tried running was Wireguard Manager with a Web UI. Using Docker, it spun up painlessly, but... it didn't work... (Yeah, I spent an entire afternoon on a nice-looking interface whose core functionality simply didn't work...)

## WireGuard

Later I gave up on that and just used WireGuard directly. I have to admit, I initially thought it would be a hassle, but once I actually used it I found the image provided by `linuxserver.io` is genuinely great—you just specify how many peers you want in `docker-compose.yml` from the start and it creates them all cleanly without fuss. Adding more peers to `docker-compose.yml` later also appends them automatically.

## Linux Client

Honestly, Android setup is super easy—you literally just scan a QR code. Linux setup, on the other hand, is relatively troublesome; every single parameter has to match for it to work.
