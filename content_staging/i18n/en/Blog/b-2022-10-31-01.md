# KDE

## Stereotypes About KDE

Back around the KDE 3.x era, I used it once because it looked pretty, and from then on I had a long-standing stereotype:

> `KDE` is a very resource-heavy desktop...

Because back then I remember comparing it to `Windows Vista/7`... Indeed, KDE with all visual effects turned on was a very heavy DE.

## Manjaro XFCE

The reason I'm bringing up Manjaro XFCE is that when I started seriously using a Linux Desktop, I started with Manjaro XFCE.. At first I didn't dare think about using the KDE Edition because I was afraid my VM couldn't handle it. Only later, when I had a physical machine capable of running it and wanted a prettier look... did I give the KDE edition a try.

## The Astonishing KDE

Honestly, I was mentally prepared for the machine to run slower, only to find there didn't seem to be any difference... Though in terms of flashiness it doesn't look quite as wildly imaginative as GNOME (yes, KDE is a sensible, down-to-earth homemaker... ).

## Performance Differences

This goes back to my VNC machine. I switched through a few DEs (initially because I thought XFCE wasn't pretty enough) and found that every single one I switched to felt heavy...

Until I switched back to the KDE edition. After stripping out some things a VNC host doesn't need, I found it actually runs super smoothly (idle RAM about 600~800MB, around XFCE usage)... On top of that, `xrdp`-related configs mysteriously refused to run properly on XFCE (probably just because I hadn't written the config file right), plus Manjaro doesn't make installing `lxqt` very convenient... (Now that Manjaro Architect isn't supported by default anymore, it's pretty hard to install... non-prepackaged desktop environments.)

# Conclusion

I love KDE... even though GNOME looks prettier and dresses up nicer.

I love KDE... even though backing up desktop settings means having to `cp` a pile of files that look messy, with no `dconf` available.

GNOME is a flashy, seductive siren.. Sure, it's glamorous and catches the eye, but KDE is the one you can actually settle down and live a good life with.

(The above remarks represent personal impressions only, with no intention to mislead or disparage GNOME enthusiasts...)
