# Desires

## TV

At the end of last year, I finally decided to get a TV for the living room, because I really couldn't stand the whole family cramming into my study to watch movies together on a single 32" monitor...
After thinking it over again and again, I bought this cheap 86" TV.

![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Kiwi0093/graph/master/img/000001_1737016623.webp)

## TV BOX

After using the TV's built-in Google TV for a while, I noticed that videos with external/separate subtitle files easily suffered from subtitle out-of-sync issues and the like. So the beast inside couldn't hold back anymore, and I bought what is currently hailed as the most powerful TV BOX — the `Nvidia Shield TV Pro 2019`.
Yes, even though it's from 2019, it somehow still has the best performance on the market... Makes you wonder whether the hardware was overkill back then or other brands are just being stingy with specs...

![|640](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Kiwi0093/graph/master/img/000001_1739785749.webp)

### Thoughts & Impressions

* Damn expensive — It actually cost over 6k NTD. Even the latest Google TV Streamer 4K (a 2024 product) only costs around 3k+ NTD, yet this five-plus-year-old piece of hardware still sells for this much...
* The SoC is genuinely fast — I looked up the Nvidia Tegra X1+ (the same chip as the Nintendo Switch... roughly 5th-gen i5 level performance). It's about twice as fast as the MediaTek chip in the Google Streamer 4K (though power draw is triple at 15W).
* 4K AI Upscaling — Actually, this feature was the main reason I bought it, and it's also its biggest selling point. However, in initial use my untrained eyes can't tell a huge difference yet... Plus not every app supports it (for example, Duboku doesn't trigger Upscaling), though maybe I just haven't configured it right—I'll try again soon.
* Gaming stream — Since I don't use `Geforce Now`, I can't comment on it, but I have used `moonlight` to stream my Steam games (Ace Combat 7). It looks pretty decent and pairs smoothly with a Bluetooth controller.

The next step will probably be gradually turning it into part of an entertainment center without messing up Android (besides Emby which I already use, maybe streaming games via moonlight... or running something like an emulator collection).

## Gaming Server (Under Consideration)

Lately I've been stuck in a `To be or Not to be` state...
It started because what I really want is a laptop with a dedicated GPU to run a Linux host (since right now, outside of the Steam Deck's desktop mode, I don't have a physical Linux terminal to use), and I'd really like to set up a penguin machine as a daily driver (a toy that can actually get work done).
Considering that buying a brand-new laptop outright doesn't seem as cost-effective as building a new Gaming Server and freeing up the gaming laptop currently filling that role to repurpose it, I dove headfirst into researching desktop builds.
Here's what makes it tough:

* Haven't followed desktop builds and specs in ages — Since 2011, I haven't bought any desktop that wasn't meant to be a server; I switched completely to laptops. My shopping habits also changed from going to Guanghua Digital Plaza to buy parts to browsing `PChome`, ordering online, and waiting for delivery at home... Going back to DIY feels really unfamiliar now, and I have no mental baseline left.
* Gap between budget and expectations — My mental model was still stuck in the era when laptops cost more than desktops. After looking around, I have to admit that the PMs behind every laptop spec actually worked hard to balance the configuration... Looking at desktops makes it way too easy to fall into the trap of "just spend a bit more for better specs," only to blow past the budget by the end—yet sticking to the budget leaves specs feeling slightly lacking and hard to swallow.
  After all that, it feels like only winning the lottery could solve it.... =_=!!
  
It's gotten to the point where I'm thinking I might as well put that money into stocks instead... That'd probably give a better return...
Besides, the moment I turn on my computer, two kids pull chairs up on my left and right, squeezing into my 120cm-wide desk and asking "Daddy, what are you doing..." It really kills any urge to buy one...
