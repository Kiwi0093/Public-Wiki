## Basic Concepts

### Components

Regardless of which platform you use, it's mainly achieved by having the following tools divide up the work:

* [jackett](https://github.com/Jackett/Jackett) - Responsible for searching on Indexers and sending the results back.
* [sonarr](https://sonarr.tv/) - This is the core component. After subscribing to the TV shows you want via TVDB, it sends commands to Jackett to look for torrents, then passes the found info to the downloader to download, and finally renames and organizes the downloaded files neatly (to match Emby's formatting requirements).
* [radarr](https://radarr.video/) - Basically has the same functionality as sonarr, except this one specializes in movies. So just as it's recommended to run separate sonarr instances for TV shows and anime, for movies you run another radarr instance.
* [qBittorrent](https://www.qbittorrent.org/) - Basically a BitTorrent downloader; used mainly because sonarr can control its downloads directly through its WebUI interface.
* [Emby](https://emby.media/) - This one is actually optional; its real purpose is just to give your media library a pretty UI so any device can watch by account. Simply put, having it just makes things look a bit more high-end.
* [Chinesesubfinder](https://github.com/allanpk716/ChineseSubFinder) - A tool that automatically finds Chinese subtitles for you.

### Basic Requirements

Since it automatically downloads to fill up your media library, there are mainly two things to satisfy:

* Plenty of storage space - Whether it's storage for the media library or temporary space for BT downloads, both eat up a lot of disk space. Especially for BT temp storage, if possible it's best to use a disposable HDD you don't mind wearing out to extend the lifespan of your media library drives.
* A host that can stay powered on long-term - This is also why most tutorials say to use NAS + Docker; after all, a NAS is definitely running 24/7, making it the best fit for this.

### Brief Workflow Explanation

Once configured, you just add the TV shows or movies you want to subscribe to in the Sonarr/Radarr interface and leave it alone. Whatever is available on the Indexers will be automatically queued in the downloader, then you wait for it to finish downloading, it'll automatically rename the files, wait a bit more and it'll find and place the subtitles on its own, and then you'll see it show up on your Emby.

### Caveats

#### Profile

In sonarr/radarr's profile settings, you define how high of a video resolution to grab. All I can say here is don't get greedy. At first I set it to maximum 4K quality, and a single movie turned out to be 70GB. Unless you have ridiculously huge storage, stay within your means—generally setting it around 1080p webrip puts a movie at roughly 2~5GB.

#### indexer

Generally I use RARBG for US TV shows and movies, and whatever else depending on mood. For anime I use DMHY, though friends in mainland China should double-check whether these indexers are blocked by the firewall—if so, remember to route the machine that Jackett connects from through a proxy/VPN.

#### Manual Downloads

I basically download anime manually, and Chinese dramas too (since these tools have weaker support for both). For manually downloaded files, just put them under the show/movie's main folder defined in sonarr/radarr and then use their manual import feature to import and organize them, and you're all set.

#### Other

The various components mentioned above can actually be split to run on separate independent machines, just remember that `/download` and `/media` must be directly controllable by sonarr/radarr. Beyond that, I suggest you give it a try first and come back to discuss if you run into any issues (even using TeamViewer to help troubleshoot is totally fine).
