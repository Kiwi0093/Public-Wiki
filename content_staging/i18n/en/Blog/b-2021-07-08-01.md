Anynode, which I rented back when I was still in Suzhou, seems to have been sold, so I got an email notifying me that the service was being migrated to BuyVM. After two or three days of tinkering, I finally got the new VPS service up and running today...

# BuyVM/Frantech

## VPS Plans

[Plan Page](https://my.frantech.ca/cart.php?gid=39)

![AMD Ryzen KVM|60](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Kiwi0093/graph/master/img/buyvm.PNG)

For an unmetered-bandwidth VPS, <font  color="green">$2.00USD</font> a month—or only <font  color="green">$19.67USD</font> if paid annually—is a really generous price, and they even offer a Luxembourg server outside DMCA jurisdiction. On top of that, they also offer an attachable <font  color="orange">Block Storage</font> service: for a little extra each month, you get a pretty sizable amount of capacity to use.

![Block Storage|60](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Kiwi0093/graph/master/img/block_storage.PNG)

If you want to do... whatever with it, it's definitely pretty handy.

As for speed, honestly I have no idea, since I'm no longer physically on a network in <ruby><del>the Celestial Empire</del><rt style="color:orange">the neighbor across the strait</rt></ruby> to test it myself, so I can only ask friends still over there to help test.

Still, if someone wants to host their own site but lacks the hardware and bandwidth, it's worth considering—for under $5 USD a month you get plenty of storage to work with.

## Server Setup

This VPS finally shows some real effort: instead of the SolusVM interface, it actually looks like the real deal, and the machine itself allows installing Windows or custom ISOs. That said, I tried Arch Linux and it didn't seem to boot very smoothly, so I just went with the default Debian option.

The control panel is called Stallion, and it looks much classier than the management panels used by previous budget VPS providers. Hopefully this turns out to be a solid provider across every metric.



## Anynode Migration

Now for the main point: because Anynode was sold, the way they handled the migration to BuyVM was pretty interesting. Instead of directly giving me two ready-to-use VPS instances (yes, I had two Anynode VPSes under my name), they prorated the remaining time on both instances at BuyVM's current rates and refunded it into my credit account. My two old Anynode boxes were valid until November this year, and originally cost only $21 USD each for two years—the remaining balance converted to a total of <font  color="green">$28.05USD</font>, which actually works out to a pretty sweet deal.



## Status of Other VPSes

Since coming back, without any real use for them, that whole pile of VPSes I held has indeed been slowly withering away—expiring or going unrenewed. Still, this week I did go through and tidy up the machines that are still alive. I originally wanted to run things with Docker, though looking closely, those VPSes seem to be OpenVZ or KVM, so I'm not sure if Docker will run into issues.
