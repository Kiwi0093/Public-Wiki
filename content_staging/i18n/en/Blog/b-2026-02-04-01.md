# Adjustments

## Lidarr

### Incident

The root cause was that `Musicbrainz` changed its API-related settings, causing `lidarr` to fail to retrieve metadata from `Musicbrainz` properly, which basically rendered `lidarr` useless.

### Countermeasure

Simply put, the ultimate solution was to <ruby><font color="lightgreen" size="+3">wait</font></ruby>. Once you waited long enough, the `lidarr` dev team actually released an updated version of `lidarr` to work with `Musicbrainz`'s new settings (though the text search part is still beyond saving).
<ruby><font color="red" size="+3">BUT</font></ruby> since everyone couldn't wait earlier, before the dev team fixed it, people started using modified builds or adding mods. As a result, those who used a modified build found that upgrading directly to the new official version didn't work either—which led to the painful ordeal that followed.

### The Tortuous Process

Because this old guy <ruby><font color="lightblue"><del>proactively adopted the best solution available at the time</del></font><rp>(</rp><rt><font color="red">couldn't wait six or seven months and got itchy fingers</font></rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, after the update I had no choice but to <ruby><font color="red" size="+3">completely wipe</font></ruby> the entire `lidarr` config, then reconfigure everything and re-import the files... Yeah... that process really was long and exhausting...



### Differences Between Old and New

The biggest difference is that the fixed `lidarr` still cannot use `text search` like before to search directly by artist name or album name and `add new`. Instead, you have to go to `Musicbrainz` first to look up the artist's `Artist ID` or the album's `Release group ID`.

Then go into `lidarr`'s `add new` and use:

```bash
lidarr:<artist ID/Release group ID>
```

And just like before, select the cover that pops up and add it into `lidarr`.

## Networking

### Automedia Setup Adjustments

Originally, based on reference guides, I wrapped the entire automedia stack (including `radarr`, `sonarr`, `lidarr`, `jackett`...) inside `Gluten`. However, `Gluten`'s VPN has been acting up lately (honestly, I'm not even sure how long it's been broken).
First I noticed `jackett` + `flaresolverr` were acting weird, so I moved that pair out of `Gluten`.
Then while fixing `lidarr` (yep, the one mentioned above...), I found that `add new` search couldn't connect... After decisively moving `lidarr` out of `Gluten`, it worked immediately...
So I moved everything in the entire automedia stack out of `Gluten` except `qbittorrent`. After all, that matches my ideal setup anyway—only `qbittorrent` goes through the VPN to stay safe, while the other services don't waste VPN bandwidth...
And then I realized the issue mentioned in the reference guides—that they wouldn't be able to talk to each other if not placed together—had disappeared... (probably because I deliberately assigned static IPs to every container in automedia...)

### VPN - ERROR [vpn] getting public IP address information:

Just add `PUBLICIP_ENABLED=false` under `enviroment` in `docker-compose.yml` and it's fixed.
