# Introduction

A couple of days ago, my long-uncontacted master brought bad news:
"G Suite is going to start charging money, run while you can~"
For someone like me who has tons of accounts and bindings tied to the email address my master gave me, this was a bolt from the blue....

<!--truncate-->

# Changing Email Addresses

## Final Deadline

### The free edition of G Suite will officially shut down after 2022/6/30

## Impact

Even though I have lots of bindings, the ones with the biggest stakes—banks—were actually the easiest to update (after all, those are tied to a natural person, so as long as the country doesn't change my national ID number, I'm not screwed).

Rented VPS accounts are all pretty troublesome, though most of the servers won't be renewed anyway so it's not actually that bad (actually I just forgot to change those...), except....

# Cloudflare

I basically finished changing the account so I can bind a different email address, but in the process I messed up a huge bunch of corresponding SSL keys.

The auto-update SSL key feature really is a double-edged sword—the moment DNS settings weren't configured properly, the domain's corresponding SSL key broke.... I have to wait anywhere from a week to three months for it to be released before I can use it again... =_=!!

On top of that, I happened to discover that domains with the proxy feature enabled can't have too many layers or things break... Adding this to my to-do list for now.

# Oracle Cloud

This one is a tragedy that cannot be changed... Looks like I can only keep using it as long as I never forget the password.....

# Zoho Mail

As an alternative, the only one I've found so far is this—an email service that supports custom domains, with up to 5 free mailboxes....

Who knows how long "free" will actually stay free though....

# Conclusion

Anyway, while there's still a free option available, I'll use it first. If it really stops working, I'll just have to host my own Mail Server. Good thing setting one up with Docker nowadays is super convenient—as long as you have a server, that is....
