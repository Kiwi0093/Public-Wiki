# HomeLab Architecture Overview

> <img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/Status-Active-A8FF24?style=for-the-badge&labelWidth=100" height="38" />

This represents the architecture of my HomeLab, continuously refined over the years, reflecting the current equilibrium among requirements, electricity costs, and hardware expenses. It is archived here for reference, and anyone interested in setting up a similar architecture is welcome to exchange ideas.

---

## 1. Design Tenets

* **Minimum Actual Cost**  
  Considering budget and electricity bills, it is impractical to run family services across a fleet of physical machines. The physical foundation of the entire setup essentially consists of **one Tower Server + one legacy physical NAS + a complimentary OCI Free Tier cloud instance**.
* **Maximum Functional Atomization**  
  Having suffered through the early pain of stuffing various services onto a single host—resulting in tangled dependencies and debugging nightmares—I have fully adopted containerization and applied strict isolation to hosts based on security levels and usage requirements.
* **Satisfying Digital Hoarding**  
  I am a digital hoarder. Lacking the funds to stockpile enterprise-grade hardware every day, I stockpile digital data. Therefore, the overall architecture might not be pristine or minimalist, but capacity and data classification are definitely well-covered.
* **Satisfying Quirky Vanity**  
  I am aware there are many out-of-the-box turnkey solutions or commercial software options available to easily fulfill requirements, but unfortunately, I am an old-school person living in the 2000s: **If I can hardcode it with FreeBSD, I will never buy a ready-made router; if I can hardcode it with Linux, I will never pay for a subscription service**.
