## Current Status

The current status is that I don't have any systems running Manjaro, but every one of my Arch Linux desktops has `manjaro-zsh-config`—a package that has been removed from the AUR but whose Git repo is still retained...
Manjaro's impact on me is profound, especially in shaping what I think a Linux desktop should look like, much like how many people encountered Windows 98 the first time they used a computer when young, leaving a lifelong impression that computer operating systems should look that way.

## Characteristics

Manjaro was one of the earliest Arch-based distributions specialized for the desktop, featuring many useful small tools and interface embellishments. Personally, I feel that compared to other subsequent Arch-based distros, it is still very excellent, so when useful tools from the AUR catch my eye, I still want to bring them into Arch.

## Pain Points

It is actually not much of a problem. Previously, rumors online talked about infighting and arguments causing supporters to churn. I can only say that when love fades, it fades—no need for so many excuses, and even when love fades, one should fairly evaluate its pros and cons.
From my perspective, I can only say that switching to Arch Linux was for a cleaner system. This is actually quite contradictory: as a newcomer, I loved how Manjaro took care of everything for me, but once familiar, I complained that it meddled too much and pre-installed too many unnecessary things...
It really feels like a scumbag behavior.

Therefore, to commemorate the good times using Manjaro, I have kept all my past related notes intact—sort of as a nostalgic keepsake.
